Paragraph Styles

Tight line-height (1.2)

Lines set close together feel dense and can tire the eye over long passages. They suit short captions, not articles.

Comfortable line-height (1.7)

Generous spacing between lines gives each line room to breathe. For body text, a line-height between 1.5 and 1.8 is usually the most readable.

Alignment

Left-aligned text is the default and is easiest to read in English.

Centred text works for short quotes and titles.

Right-aligned text suits captions or dates.

Justified text stretches each line to fill the full width, giving clean edges on both sides. It looks tidy in columns but can leave uneven gaps between words when lines are short.

Text Emphasis

Use bold for importance, italics for stress, underline sparingly (it looks like a link), strikethrough for removed ideas, overline for decoration, and wide letter-spacing for short labels.

Combine them with care: bold, italic and tight spacing draws attention, but too many styles at once weakens all of them.

Good typography is invisible; bad typography is everywhere.
